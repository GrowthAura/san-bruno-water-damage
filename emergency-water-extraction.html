How fast should standing water be removed from a San Bruno home?

Within the first few hours. Water starts wicking into drywall within minutes, and mold can begin in 24 to 72 hours, faster in San Bruno's foggy, humid neighborhoods.

The first hours after a leak decide how big the job becomes. Water that sits on a floor moves sideways under baseboards, soaks into carpet pad, seeps between hardwood boards and climbs drywall like a sponge. In a raised home it drips through the subfloor into the crawl space below. The faster the bulk water comes out, the less material has to be cut out and replaced, and the shorter the drying time.

Equipment Used for Water Extraction

The right tool depends on how much water there is and where it sits.

Truck-Mounted Extraction Units

Powerful units that stay outside in the truck and pull large volumes of water through long hoses. These are used for whole-floor losses, flooded garages and big commercial spaces.

Portable Extractors

Smaller units that can be carried upstairs, into condos, or into tight spaces like hallways and closets where a truck hose cannot reach easily.

Submersible Pumps

Used when water is several inches deep, such as in a flooded garage, crawl space or below-grade room.

Weighted Carpet Extraction Tools

Tools the technician stands on to press water out of carpet and pad, pulling far more water than a wand alone.

Moisture Meters and Thermal Cameras

Used during and after extraction to find water that moved under cabinets, behind baseboards and into walls.

Where Water Hides in San Bruno Homes

Most homes in San Bruno were built between the 1940s and 1960s. That age shapes where water ends up after a leak.

Under Carpet and Pad

Wall-to-wall carpet over old pad holds a surprising amount of water. Extraction pulls most of it, but wet pad usually needs to be replaced.

Beneath Raised Floors

Many ranch homes in Crestmoor, Rollingwood and the western hills sit over crawl spaces. Water that soaks through the floor collects on the soil below and keeps the house damp for weeks unless it is pumped and dried. More on that is in the crawl space water damage guide.

In Garages and Ground-Level Units

Garages, converted garages and in-law units sit at grade. In storms, runoff from the street and driveway pools here first.

Behind Cabinets and Walls

Kitchen and bathroom cabinets trap water under their bases. Drywall wicks water upward several inches above the visible water line.

Extraction by Surface Type

Carpet

Carpet soaked with clean water can often be extracted, dried in place and saved. The pad underneath is usually removed. Carpet that touched sewage or outside floodwater must be removed for health reasons.

Hardwood

Hardwood needs fast extraction followed by specialized drying mats to pull water out of the boards before they cup. See the hardwood floor water damage page.

Tile and Vinyl

Water on tile or vinyl looks easy to clean, but it often slips through grout lines and seams into the subfloor underneath, so moisture readings are taken after extraction.

Concrete

Garage and slab floors are extracted and squeegeed, then dried with dehumidifiers so moisture does not move into walls and stored items.

What Happens After Extraction

Extraction removes the bulk water, but the materials stay wet inside. The next step is structural drying with air movers and dehumidifiers, checked every day with moisture readings. In San Bruno's cool, foggy climate this step matters even more because the air does not dry things on its own. Learn more on the structural drying page.

Clean Water, Gray Water and Sewage

The source of the water changes how extraction is done:

  • Clean water from a supply line or rain is extracted and dried, with most materials often saved.
  • Gray water from appliances or a toilet overflow without waste needs extraction plus cleaning and sanitizing.
  • Black water, including sewage and outside floodwater, needs protective gear, removal of porous materials and disinfection. See sewage backup cleanup.

What to Do Before the Crew Arrives

  • Shut off the main water valve if a pipe or fixture is the source.
  • Turn off power to wet rooms at the breaker if you can do so safely.
  • Lift furniture off wet carpet and put foil or wood blocks under the legs.
  • Move rugs, electronics, papers and photos to a dry area.
  • Take photos and video of the water before cleanup begins.
  • Do not use a household vacuum on standing water.
  • Stay out of sewage and floodwater.

Emergency Water Extraction FAQs

How long does water extraction take?

+

Most single-room jobs take one to three hours. Whole-floor losses, flooded garages and crawl spaces can take longer, followed by several days of drying.

Can I just use a shop vac?

+

A shop vac helps with small spills on hard floors, but it cannot pull water out of carpet pad, subfloors or wall cavities. Standing water removal from those areas needs professional equipment.

Will my carpet be saved?

+

Carpet soaked with clean water is often dried and saved. The pad is usually replaced. Carpet touched by sewage or floodwater is removed.

Do you extract water from crawl spaces?

+

Yes. Crawl spaces are pumped out, and then dried with dehumidifiers to protect the floor framing above.

How Much Water Do Floors and Walls Actually Hold?

People are often surprised by how much water hides in a room that looks only damp. A single room of carpet and pad can hold many gallons after a leak. Drywall acts like a wick and can pull water several inches up the wall above the visible line. Wood subfloors and framing absorb water slowly, but they release it slowly too, which is why they need days of dehumidification. This is the reason extraction comes first: every gallon pulled out with equipment is a gallon that does not have to be evaporated over the next several days.

Extraction in Condos and Upper Floors

When a leak happens on an upper floor or in a condo, water rarely stays where it started. It moves through the floor into the ceiling below, along joists, and into neighboring walls. Extraction on upper floors uses portable units that can be carried up stairs, and the crew checks the rooms and units below at the same time. In buildings like the Shelter Creek condos, getting to the unit below quickly can save its ceiling and keep a one-unit loss from becoming a two-unit loss.

Signs Water Is Still Hiding After Cleanup

Even after the standing water is gone, watch for these signs that moisture is still trapped:

  • Baseboards that stay dark or swollen
  • Paint bubbling a few days after the leak
  • A musty smell that gets stronger instead of weaker
  • Floors that feel cold or soft underfoot
  • Condensation on windows in the affected room
