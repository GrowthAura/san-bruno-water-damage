How Peninsula Fog Leads to Mold and How to Prevent It

(650) 822-6798Request Emergency Help
Home› Peninsula Fog and Mold

If you live in the foggier parts of San Bruno, Pacifica, South San Francisco or Daly City, you already know the feeling: cool, damp air that hangs around for days, windows that fog up on the inside, closets that smell a little musty. That moisture is part of life on the Peninsula, and it is also one of the main reasons mold shows up in local homes. This guide explains how fog contributes to indoor mold and what you can do about it.

Does coastal fog cause mold inside San Bruno and Pacifica homes?

Fog alone rarely causes mold, but it keeps indoor humidity high. When humid air meets cold walls, windows or crawl spaces, condensation forms and mold can grow.

Mold needs three things: moisture, something to eat such as drywall paper, wood or dust, and time. Fog provides a steady supply of moisture. In the foggy northern and western parts of San Bruno, and almost everywhere in Pacifica, outdoor humidity stays high for much of the year. That humid air gets inside through vents, open windows, doors and crawl spaces. When it touches a surface colder than the air, such as an exterior wall in a closet or a single-pane window, it condenses into water. Do that day after day, and you have mold.

Where Fog-Related Mold Shows Up

Closets on Exterior Walls

Closets are closed, dark and often against cold outside walls. Clothes and boxes block airflow. Mold commonly shows up on the back wall, behind hanging clothes, and on shoes and bags.

Window Sills and Frames

Condensation on windows runs down onto sills and frames, especially older aluminum or single-pane windows.

Bathrooms

Showers add moisture to air that is already humid. Without a good fan, ceilings and corners grow mold.

Behind Furniture

Furniture pushed tight against exterior walls traps cool, damp air behind it.

Crawl Spaces

Vents let marine air under the house, and the soil holds moisture. Joists and subfloor can grow mold even without a leak.

Garages and Lower Levels

Unheated garages and rooms built into hillsides stay cool and damp, making them prime spots for mold.

Ideal Indoor Humidity on the Peninsula

Most homes do best with indoor relative humidity between about 30 and 50 percent. Above 60 percent for long stretches, mold growth becomes much more likely. An inexpensive digital hygrometer from a hardware store tells you where your home stands. Check a few rooms, including a closet and a bathroom.

Ten Ways to Prevent Fog-Related Mold

  • Run bathroom fans during showers and for at least 30 minutes after
  • Use a kitchen range hood that vents outside when cooking
  • Run a dehumidifier in closets, lower levels and rooms that feel damp, and empty or drain it regularly
  • Keep furniture a few inches away from exterior walls
  • Leave closet doors open when you can, and do not overpack closets
  • Wipe condensation off windows in the morning
  • Keep the home gently heated in winter; cold rooms collect more condensation
  • Check the crawl space for moisture and make sure vents and vapor barriers are in good shape
  • Fix leaks right away and dry wet areas within 24 hours
  • Avoid drying laundry indoors without ventilation

Dehumidifiers: What to Know

A dehumidifier is one of the best tools for foggy homes. A few tips:

  • Choose a size rated for the room or level you want to dry
  • Set it to around 45 to 50 percent humidity
  • Use a continuous drain hose if possible so it does not shut off when full
  • Clean the filter regularly
  • Place it where air can circulate, not tight against a wall

When Water Damage and Fog Combine

Fog alone usually causes surface mold that can be managed. The bigger problem comes when there is also a leak, a flood or storm water. Wet materials in humid coastal air dry very slowly. That is why water damage on the Peninsula has to be dried mechanically with dehumidifiers and checked with moisture meters, not left to air dry. Learn more on the structural drying page.

When to Call for Mold Remediation

Small spots of surface mold on a window sill or tile grout can often be cleaned by a homeowner. Call for help when:

  • Mold covers more than a small patch
  • Mold keeps coming back after cleaning
  • There is a musty smell but you cannot find the source
  • Mold appears after a leak, flood or sewage backup
  • Someone in the home has asthma, allergies or a weak immune system

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do my windows fog up on the inside?

+

Warm, humid indoor air meets the cold glass and condenses. Better ventilation and lower indoor humidity reduce it.

Can I just bleach mold?

+

Bleach can lighten surface mold on hard surfaces but does not fix the moisture problem or reach mold inside porous materials.

Should I close crawl space vents?

+

It depends on your home. Some crawl spaces do better sealed and dehumidified. Ask a professional before making changes.

How fast can mold grow after a leak?

+

Within 24 to 72 hours on wet materials, faster in humid coastal air.
