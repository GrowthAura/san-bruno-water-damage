Who pays when water leaks from the condo above in San Bruno?

It depends on where the leak started and your HOA's governing documents. Generally, the unit owner covers their unit's interior, and the HOA covers common elements like shared pipes and roofs.

In most California condo associations, responsibility follows the source. If water comes from a fixture or appliance inside one unit, that owner's policy (often called an HO-6 policy) usually handles the damage. If it comes from a common element like the roof, a main riser or shared plumbing, the HOA's master policy usually applies. Your HOA's CC&Rs spell out the details, so read them or ask your property manager. The crew does not decide responsibility, but the photos and moisture logs make it much easier for everyone to sort out.

Common Sources of Condo and Apartment Water Damage in San Bruno, CA

Apartment water damage in San Bruno, CA usually starts with:

  • A washing machine, dishwasher or water heater in the unit above
  • Overflowing toilets, showers or tubs upstairs
  • Burst or leaking supply lines inside shared walls
  • Roof leaks reaching top-floor units
  • Sewer backups affecting ground-floor units
  • Sprinkler line breaks in common areas
  • HVAC condensate leaks above ceilings

Why Unit-to-Unit Leaks Are Complicated

A unit-to-unit leak affects more than one home. Water can travel through floor and ceiling cavities, along pipes and into neighboring walls. Each unit may have different owners, tenants and insurance. That means:

  • Every affected unit has to be checked, not just the one with visible damage
  • Access needs to be coordinated with several people
  • Drying in one unit may depend on drying in another
  • Clear records are needed for each owner and the HOA

How Condo Water Damage Is Handled

Find the Source

The crew traces moisture with meters and thermal imaging, working from the visible damage back to the source unit.

Stop the Water

The source is shut off. In some buildings this means shutting off water to a stack or section.

Check Every Affected Unit

Units above, below and beside the source are checked for hidden moisture.

Extract and Dry

Standing water is removed and drying equipment is set up in each affected unit.

Document

Photos, moisture readings and drying logs are kept for each unit separately.

Repair

Drywall, ceilings, flooring and finishes are restored once dry.

For landlords, quick drying protects the property and helps tenants get back to normal. The crew works with property managers and keeps records that can be shared with all parties.

Condo Water Damage FAQs

What is an HO-6 policy?

+

An HO-6 policy is condo owners insurance. It usually covers the interior of your unit and your belongings, while the HOA master policy covers the building structure and common areas.

What if my neighbor will not let the crew in?

+

Contact your HOA or property manager. Most CC&Rs give the association access rights to stop ongoing damage.

Can you work with our property manager?

+

Yes. The crew coordinates with property managers, HOA boards and owners, and shares the same records with everyone involved.

How long does drying take in a condo?

+

Usually three to five days per unit, sometimes longer when water traveled through several floors.

Documentation Checklist for Owners and HOAs

When water moves between units, good records make every conversation easier. Here is what owners, tenants and HOA boards usually need:

Source Information

Where the water came from, which unit it started in, and what failed, such as a supply line, toilet seal or water heater.

Affected Areas

A list of every unit and common area that took on water, with photos before cleanup began.

Moisture Records

Daily moisture readings for each affected unit, showing when each area reached normal levels.

Removed Materials

A list of materials removed from each unit, such as drywall, carpet pad or baseboards, with photos.

Scope of Repairs

A written scope of repairs for each unit, so owners and the HOA can see exactly what is being restored.

The crew keeps these records separately for each unit, so nobody has to sort through someone else's information.

Common Areas, Hallways and Parking Garages

Water damage in a building does not always stay inside units. Hallway carpet, stairwells, laundry rooms, elevators and underground parking can all take on water. These spaces are usually the HOA's responsibility. Drying them quickly keeps the building usable for everyone and prevents mold from spreading into walls shared with units.

Preventing Leaks in Multi-Unit Buildings

  • Replace rubber washing machine hoses with braided steel
  • Put drain pans under water heaters and washers, especially on upper floors
  • Install leak sensors in water heater closets and under sinks
  • Inspect toilet supply lines and shower pans every few years
  • Make sure every owner knows where their unit's water shutoff is
  • Schedule roof and gutter checks before the rainy season

Water Heater Closets in Condos

Many San Bruno condos keep the water heater in a small closet inside the unit or off a balcony. When a tank fails, water spreads into hallways, carpet and the unit below before anyone notices, especially if the owner is at work. A drain pan with a line to a floor drain, a leak sensor, and replacing tanks before they reach the end of their life are the best protection. If your building has several units with water heaters of the same age, the HOA may want to plan replacements together.

When the Leak Comes From a Unit Nobody Is In

Vacant units, units for sale and units whose owners travel can leak for days unnoticed. If water is coming from a unit that nobody answers, contact building management right away. Most HOAs have emergency access rights to stop damage to other units and common areas. Shutting off water to that unit or stack quickly limits how far the damage spreads.

Rebuilding After a Unit-to-Unit Leak

Once every affected unit is dry, repairs begin. In multi-unit buildings, rebuild work often needs to match existing finishes, follow HOA rules on working hours and materials, and be coordinated so units above and below are not disturbed more than necessary. Clear scopes for each unit keep owners, tenants and the HOA on the same page.

More Condo Water Damage Questions

Do I need to tell my HOA about a leak inside my unit?

+

Yes, especially if it could affect other units or common areas. Report it in writing with photos so there is a clear record.

Can drying equipment run in an occupied unit?

+

Usually yes. Equipment is noisy and adds some warmth, but most residents stay home during drying unless sewage or extensive demolition is involved.

What if my unit is affected but I am not home?

+

Give the HOA or property manager a way to reach you and permission for access if needed. Fast access means faster drying and less damage.
