What causes the most water damage in South San Francisco homes?

Aging pipes in post-war homes, sewer lateral backups, storm runoff on hillside streets, and creek-related flooding in low areas near Colma Creek cause most South San Francisco water damage calls.

South San Francisco is a city of contrasts. West of El Camino Real, neighborhoods like Westborough, Serra Highlands and Avalon Park climb into the hills, with mid-century homes on sloped lots. Between El Camino and 101, older neighborhoods around Grand Avenue, Sunshine Gardens and Mission Road sit on flatter ground, closer to the creek. East of 101, the city turns into one of the Bay Area's biggest commercial and research districts. Each part of the city has its own water damage patterns, and knowing them helps the crew find moisture faster.

South San Francisco Neighborhoods and Their Water Damage Risks

Westborough and Serra Highlands

These hillside neighborhoods west of I-280 and along Skyline Boulevard are known for late-1950s ranch homes on sloped lots. Fog rolls in often from the ocean side, and many homes sit over crawl spaces. Common calls: crawl space water after storms, burst galvanized pipes, and mold in closets and bathrooms on cold exterior walls.

Sunshine Gardens, Buri Buri and Winston Manor

Well-established neighborhoods with homes from the 1940s through the 1960s. Water damage here usually comes from older plumbing, slab and hidden leaks, failing water heaters in garages, and sewer laterals that roots have worked into over the decades.

Downtown and Grand Avenue

The historic downtown core around Grand Avenue has some of the city's oldest buildings, including mixed-use properties with shops below and apartments above. Sewer backups, old supply lines and unit-to-unit leaks are the main concerns.

Paradise Valley and Sign Hill Area

Homes on and below Sign Hill deal with runoff coming down steep streets during storms. Garages and lower-level rooms on the downhill side take the water.

Avalon Park and Country Club Park

Mixed housing on rolling terrain. Roof leaks during wind-driven storms and crawl space moisture are common.

Areas Near Colma Creek

Colma Creek runs through the middle of the city to the Bay. Low streets near the creek channel can see standing water and drain backups during heavy rain, especially when storms line up with high tides.

East of 101: Commercial and Research Districts

The area around Oyster Point and the business parks east of 101 is packed with offices, labs, warehouses and hotels. Sprinkler breaks, roof leaks and restroom overflows in large buildings need fast, large-scale drying.

Commercial Water Damage East of 101

The commercial district east of 101 is one of the busiest business areas on the Peninsula. Water losses here are often large: a sprinkler line breaking above a lab, a roof leak over a warehouse, or a restroom overflow on an upper office floor. Commercial water damage repair South San Francisco CA businesses need starts with protecting equipment and inventory, sectioning off wet areas, and drying fast enough to limit downtime. The crew can work overnight, coordinate with property managers and facility teams, and keep detailed moisture and equipment logs for insurance and compliance.

Sensitive spaces like labs, server rooms and clean rooms need extra care. Power to wet areas is shut down, equipment is moved or protected, and drying is planned to control dust and humidity.

Colma Creek Flooding and Winter Storms

Colma Creek collects runoff from a large part of the city and from the hills to the west. During long winter storms, the creek channel rises fast. When high tides in the Bay slow its flow, Colma Creek flooding can back water into storm drains and low streets nearby. Homes and businesses close to the creek, and along low parts of the flats, are most at risk.

Before storm season:

  • Clear the storm drain in front of your property and keep gutters flowing.
  • Move belongings off garage floors.
  • Know where your main water shutoff and electrical panel are.
  • Sign up for San Mateo County emergency alerts.

If water does get in, call right away. Storm water from outside is treated as contaminated, so porous materials it touched usually need to be removed, and everything else is cleaned and disinfected before drying.

Fog, Hills and Slow Drying

Neighborhoods west of El Camino, especially Westborough, sit in the fog belt. The air is cool and damp much of the year. That means a wet wall or subfloor does not dry by itself, and mold can start sooner. Proper drying here relies on dehumidifiers and daily readings, not open windows. Homes on the sunny, eastern side of the city still benefit from the same measured approach, since hidden moisture behind walls and under floors behaves the same way.

Older Sewer Laterals in South San Francisco Homes

Many South San Francisco homes were built in the decades after World War II, and their sewer laterals are now 60 or more years old. Clay and cast iron laterals crack, sag and let roots in, especially under mature trees. The result is slow drains, gurgling toilets and, eventually, sewage coming up through the lowest drain in the house. If you notice those warning signs, have the line inspected with a camera before it backs up. If sewage has already come into the home, it needs Category 3 cleanup with full protective gear and disinfection.
