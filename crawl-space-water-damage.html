Why do crawl spaces flood in Crestmoor and Rollingwood homes after heavy rain?

These mid-century homes sit on hillside lots over crawl spaces. Storm runoff flows downhill, soaks the soil around the foundation and seeps underneath, where it pools with nowhere to drain.

Many homes in Crestmoor, Rollingwood, Portola Highlands and along Skyline Boulevard were built in the 1950s and 1960s with raised floors over a concrete perimeter foundation. The space underneath is usually bare soil. When winter storms soak the hillside, water follows gravity under the house. Add a broken downspout, a clogged foundation drain or a leaking pipe, and the crawl space becomes a shallow pond.

Signs of a Flooded Crawl Space in San Bruno, CA

You rarely see a flooded crawl space in San Bruno, CA directly. Instead, watch for:

  • A musty, earthy smell inside the house, especially after rain
  • Floors that feel soft, bouncy or cold
  • Hardwood floors starting to cup or gap
  • Higher humidity indoors, foggy windows or condensation
  • More spiders, silverfish or other pests than usual
  • Visible mold on the bottom of floor joists if you look through the access hatch
  • A sump pump running constantly, or not running at all

If you notice any of these, a quick look through the crawl space hatch with a flashlight will often show standing water or wet soil.

Why Crawl Space Water Is a Big Problem

Mold on Framing

Wet soil keeps the air under the house humid. Floor joists, subfloor and sill plates absorb that moisture and grow mold, which can spread into the living space through gaps in the floor.

Wood Rot

Framing that stays damp for months starts to rot. This weakens the floor and can lead to costly structural repairs.

Indoor Air Quality

Air from the crawl space rises into the home. Mold spores and musty odors come with it.

Damaged Floors

Hardwood floors above a wet crawl space cup and buckle. Carpet and pad hold onto the musty smell.

Pests

Damp crawl spaces attract termites, rodents and insects.

Sewage Under the House

If a drain line broke under the house, the crawl space may be contaminated with sewage. That is Category 3 water and needs protective gear, removal of contaminated soil and materials, and disinfection. See sewage backup cleanup.

Keeping Water Out of Your Crawl Space

After the dry-out, these steps help stop the problem from coming back:

  • Extend downspouts so roof water lands several feet from the foundation
  • Clean gutters before the rainy season
  • Grade soil around the house so it slopes away
  • Install or repair a perimeter drain on uphill sides
  • Add a sump pump with a battery backup in crawl spaces that flood often
  • Put down a heavy vapor barrier over the soil
  • Consider encapsulation for crawl spaces with ongoing moisture problems

Crawl Spaces and the Fog Belt

Even without flooding, crawl spaces in the foggy northern and western parts of San Bruno stay damp. Vents bring in cool, wet marine air, and the soil rarely dries out. That is why a crawl space that took on water during a storm needs mechanical drying, not just time. More on fog and moisture is in the fog and mold guide.

Crawl Space FAQs

How do I know if my crawl space has water?

+

Check for musty smells, soft floors and high indoor humidity. Look through the access hatch with a flashlight after heavy rain.

Can water in the crawl space cause mold upstairs?

+

Yes. Moist air from under the house rises into the home and can lead to mold on floors, walls and furniture.

How long does crawl space drying take?

+

Usually three to seven days, depending on how much water there was and how wet the soil and framing are.

What is crawl space encapsulation?

+

It means sealing the crawl space with a heavy liner on the floor and walls and controlling humidity with a dehumidifier. It is a long-term fix for crawl spaces that stay damp.

Is crawl space water covered by insurance?

+

Water from a sudden pipe burst often is. Water from surface runoff or long-term seepage often is not. Check your policy.

Crawl Space Vapor Barriers Explained

A vapor barrier is a sheet of heavy plastic laid over the soil in a crawl space. It slows moisture rising from the ground into the air under your house. In many older San Bruno homes the original barrier is thin, torn, missing, or was never installed. After a flood, a damaged barrier usually has to be removed so the soil can dry, then replaced with a heavier one. Seams should be overlapped and taped, and the edges run up the foundation walls a few inches.

Sump Pumps for San Bruno Crawl Spaces

If your crawl space takes on water in most heavy storms, a sump pump can remove it before it pools. A good setup includes:

  • A sump pit at the lowest point of the crawl space
  • A pump sized for the amount of water you get
  • A discharge line that carries water well away from the foundation
  • A battery backup so it keeps working if the power goes out in a storm
  • An alarm or phone alert if the water level rises too high

Test the pump before the rainy season by pouring water into the pit and making sure it turns on.

Crawl Space Water and Selling Your Home

Home inspectors check crawl spaces, and standing water, mold on joists or wood rot will show up in the report. If you plan to sell, dealing with crawl space water ahead of time is usually easier than negotiating repairs during escrow. Keep the drying records and photos from any crawl space cleanup so you can show buyers the problem was handled properly.

Hillside Drainage and Crawl Spaces

On sloped lots in Crestmoor, Rollingwood and Portola Highlands, water often enters from the uphill side of the foundation. A perimeter drain, sometimes called a French drain, along that side can catch water underground and carry it around the house before it reaches the crawl space. It is one of the most effective long-term fixes for hillside homes.

Should You Go Into a Wet Crawl Space Yourself?

It is fine to look through the access hatch with a flashlight. Going all the way in is a different matter. Wet crawl spaces can hide sewage, sharp debris, live wiring, mold and pests, and the tight space makes it hard to move. If you see standing water, smell sewage, or notice wiring near the water, stay out and call for help. The crew enters with protective gear, lights and meters, and can tell you exactly what is going on under your home with photos.

How Often to Check Your Crawl Space

Check through the hatch at least once before the rainy season, again after the first big storm, and after any long run of storms. A quick look takes a few minutes and can catch water before it becomes a mold problem.
