Why do downhill homes in San Bruno get water in the garage during storms?

Garages and in-law units on downhill lots sit at the lowest point. Street runoff, driveway water and saturated hillside soil all flow toward them, entering under doors and through slab joints.

Much of San Bruno is built on slopes. In Crestmoor, Rollingwood, Portola Highlands and Pacific Heights, many homes have a garage under the house or at street level, with the living space above or behind. When a storm drops heavy rain on the hills, water runs down streets and driveways and finds the lowest opening. That is usually the garage door, a side door, or the seam where the slab meets the foundation wall.

In-Law Unit Water Damage in San Bruno, CA

Converted garages and in-law units have become common across the Peninsula, but most were built inside spaces never designed to stay dry. In-law unit water damage in San Bruno, CA is especially hard because:

  • The floor is often a concrete slab right at ground level
  • Walls may have been framed directly against the original garage walls
  • There may be no gutters, drains or grading to steer water away
  • Carpet and laminate over concrete trap moisture underneath
  • Someone may be living there, so the work has to move quickly

When water gets into a rented unit, the tenant needs a dry, safe place, and the owner needs clear records of what happened. The crew keeps photos and moisture logs for both.

How Hillside Runoff Gets Into the House

Hillside runoff into the house usually comes through a few predictable paths:

  • Under the garage door when the driveway slopes toward the house
  • Through cracks and cold joints in the slab and foundation
  • Through weep holes and vents near ground level
  • From overflowing gutters dumping water right at the foundation
  • Through window wells and below-grade windows
  • Up through floor drains when the storm system backs up

What Gets Damaged in a Flooded Garage

Stored Belongings

Boxes, papers, photos, clothing, tools and holiday decorations on the floor soak up water fast. Plastic bins and items on shelves usually survive.

Drywall and Insulation

Finished garages with drywall wick water upward. Insulation behind it holds moisture against the framing.

Water Heaters and Furnaces

Many San Bruno homes have the water heater and furnace in the garage. Water touching gas appliances, burners or electrical parts needs to be checked before use.

Laundry Equipment

Washers and dryers in the garage can be damaged if water reaches the motor or controls.

Flooring in Converted Spaces

Carpet, laminate and vinyl plank over concrete hold water underneath and often need to be lifted.

How a Flooded Garage Is Cleaned Up

Pump-Out

Standing water is removed with submersible pumps and wet vacuums.

Sorting Belongings

Items are moved out, sorted into what can be cleaned and what has to go, and photographed for your records.

Removing Wet Materials

Wet drywall, insulation, carpet and pad are removed. In in-law units, flooring is lifted to dry the slab underneath.

Cleaning and Disinfecting

Storm water is usually contaminated, so floors and walls are cleaned and disinfected.

Drying

Dehumidifiers and air movers dry the slab, framing and walls. See structural drying.

Repairs

Drywall, baseboards and flooring are replaced so the space is usable again.

Keeping Water Out Next Time

  • Install a trench drain across the bottom of a sloped driveway
  • Add or repair weatherstripping at the bottom of the garage door
  • Extend downspouts away from the garage
  • Seal cracks in the slab and at the foundation joint
  • Store belongings on shelves or in sealed plastic bins, not on the floor
  • Raise water heaters and appliances on platforms
  • Keep sandbags on hand for storm warnings

Garage Flooding and Mold

A garage that floods and dries slowly in San Bruno's damp climate is a prime spot for mold. Mold grows on drywall paper, cardboard boxes and wood framing within a few days. If you smell mustiness after a garage flood, the area may need mold remediation.

Flooded Garage FAQs

Is a flooded garage covered by insurance?

+

Water from outside runoff is often excluded from homeowners policies, while a burst pipe or failed water heater in the garage is often covered. Check your policy.

Can a converted garage be dried without tearing everything out?

+

Sometimes. If water was shallow and caught quickly, flooring can be lifted, the slab dried and walls dried with injection. Contaminated water usually means more removal.

Should my tenant leave during cleanup?

+

If the unit's floor and walls are wet, it is usually best for the tenant to stay elsewhere until drying is done, typically a few days.

Is it safe to use my water heater after the garage flooded?

+

Not until it is checked. If water reached the burner, controls or electrical parts, have it inspected before using it.

Gas Appliances and Electrical Panels in a Flooded Garage

Garages in San Bruno often hold the water heater, the furnace, the washer and dryer, and sometimes the main electrical panel. Water and these systems are a dangerous mix. If water has reached any of them:

  • Do not turn on or relight a gas water heater or furnace until it has been checked
  • Do not touch the electrical panel if you are standing in water
  • Have a qualified technician inspect gas controls, burners and wiring that got wet
  • Replace electrical outlets and switches that were submerged

The restoration crew can shut off power to wet areas and work around these systems safely, but the appliances themselves should be checked by the right trade before use.

Sorting Belongings After a Garage Flood

Garages are where families store the things they do not use every day: photos, holiday decorations, tools, paperwork, sports gear. Sorting them quickly saves more.

Usually Saved

Metal tools, plastic bins with lids, glass, ceramic and sealed containers can be cleaned and disinfected.

Often Saved With Care

Photos and important papers can sometimes be saved if they are frozen or air-dried quickly. Wooden furniture can often be cleaned and dried.

Usually Discarded

Cardboard boxes, mattresses, upholstered items, carpet remnants and anything soft that soaked in storm water.

Renting Out an In-Law Unit After Water Damage

If you rent out a converted garage or in-law unit, water damage affects your tenant right away. Make sure the unit is fully dried, cleaned and clear of mold before anyone moves back in. Keep the drying logs and photos with your rental records. They show the space was restored properly, which protects both you and your tenant.

Garage Floor Coatings and Water

Epoxy and other floor coatings make garage floors easier to clean and help water sit on top of the slab rather than soaking in. They do not stop water from entering under the door, but they make cleanup faster and reduce staining and odors. If your garage floods often, a coating combined with a trench drain at the driveway and better door seals can make each storm much less of a problem.

Converted Garages and Moisture From Below

Even without a flood, converted garages can feel damp because the slab sits right on the ground with no barrier underneath. Flooring laid directly on the slab can trap that moisture and grow mold. A moisture test on the slab before new flooring goes down, and a proper vapor barrier or underlayment, help keep a converted space dry for the long term.

Garage Flooding FAQ for Landlords

Who pays when a rented in-law unit floods?

+

It depends on the cause, the lease and your insurance. Building repairs are usually the owner's responsibility, while tenants often need renters insurance for their belongings.

How soon can a tenant move back in?

+

Usually once drying is complete, materials are back to normal moisture levels, and any contaminated materials have been removed and replaced, which often takes several days to a week.

What records should I keep?

+

Photos, moisture logs, a list of removed materials and the repair scope. These help with insurance and show the unit was restored properly.
