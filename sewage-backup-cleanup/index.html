Who is responsible for a sewer lateral backup in San Bruno?

The property owner. In San Bruno, the private sewer lateral running from your house to the city main is the owner's responsibility, including repairs and replacement.

That surprises a lot of homeowners. The lateral is the pipe that carries everything from your toilets, sinks and showers out to the public sewer in the street. In older San Bruno homes it is often clay or cast iron, decades old, and full of cracks where tree roots get in. When it clogs, sewage has nowhere to go but back up into the lowest drain in the house.

What Causes a Sewer Backup in San Bruno, CA?

The most common causes of a sewer backup in San Bruno, CA:

  • Tree roots growing into old clay or cast iron laterals
  • Cracked, sagging or broken lateral pipes
  • Grease, wipes and paper products clogging the line
  • Heavy storms overloading the sewer system
  • High water in low areas near San Bruno Creek pushing water back through drains
  • A blockage in the city main, which is the city's responsibility

The Point-of-Sale Sewer Lateral Rule

San Bruno requires a sewer lateral inspection when a home is sold. If the inspection finds problems, the lateral has to be repaired or replaced, and the cost falls on the owner. If you are buying or selling an older home, this is worth planning for. The sewer lateral guide explains the rule in plain terms.

Why Sewage Is Category 3 Water

The IICRC S500 standard classifies sewage as Category 3, the most contaminated type of water. It can carry E. coli, hepatitis, norovirus and other pathogens. Category 3 water cleanup follows strict rules:

  • Porous materials it touched, like carpet, pad, drywall and insulation, are removed and bagged
  • Hard surfaces are cleaned and disinfected
  • Workers wear protective suits, gloves, boots and respirators
  • The area is contained so contamination does not spread to clean rooms
  • Drying happens only after contaminated materials are removed

Ground-Floor Bathrooms and Laundry Rooms

The lowest drain in the house is usually where sewage shows up first.

Crawl Spaces

In raised homes, a broken drain line under the house can leak sewage directly onto the soil. It often goes unnoticed until the smell rises. See crawl space water damage.

Garages and In-Law Units

Converted garages and ground-level units often sit on the lowest drains in the home.

Low-Lying Neighborhoods

In Belle Air and other areas near San Bruno Creek, heavy storms and high tides can push water back through floor drains. See storm and flood damage.

Backwater Valves and Prevention

A backwater valve on the lateral lets sewage flow out but stops it from flowing back into the house. In low areas and older homes it is one of the best ways to prevent repeat backups. Other steps that help:

  • Do not flush wipes, even ones labeled flushable
  • Keep grease out of kitchen drains
  • Have the lateral inspected with a camera if you have had slow drains or a past backup
  • Know where your sewer cleanout is so a plumber can access it quickly

What to Do Right Now

  • Stop using water in the house: no flushing, showers or laundry.
  • Keep everyone, especially children and pets, away from the area.
  • Do not touch sewage with bare hands.
  • Turn off power to the area if it is safe to reach the panel.
  • Open windows if it is safe, to reduce odors.

Sewage Backup FAQs

Is it safe to clean up sewage myself?

+

It is not recommended. Sewage carries harmful bacteria and viruses, and porous materials it touched must be removed, not just cleaned.

Does homeowners insurance cover sewer backups?

+

Many policies exclude sewer backup unless you have a specific endorsement for it. Check your policy. The crew documents everything for your claim either way.

Can carpet be saved after a sewage backup?

+

No. Carpet and pad that touched sewage have to be removed and disposed of.

How long does sewage cleanup take?

+

Cleanup and disinfection usually take one to two days, followed by several days of drying before repairs.

Who fixes the sewer line?

+

A plumber repairs or replaces the lateral. The restoration crew handles the cleanup, disinfection, drying and repairs inside the home.

Health Risks of Sewage Exposure

Sewage contains bacteria, viruses and parasites that can cause stomach illness, skin infections and other problems. Children, older adults, pregnant women and anyone with a weak immune system are most at risk. That is why it is important to keep people and pets away from the affected area until cleanup is done, and to avoid touching contaminated water or materials without gloves and boots. If anyone feels sick after contact with sewage, talk to a doctor.

What Can Be Saved After a Sewage Backup

Not everything has to be thrown out. Here is a general guide:

  • Usually saved: hard, non-porous items such as metal, glass, sealed plastic, ceramic tile and solid hardwood furniture that can be cleaned and disinfected
  • Sometimes saved: clothing and fabrics that can be laundered in hot water, depending on how long they were exposed
  • Usually removed: carpet, pad, mattresses, upholstered furniture, cardboard, paper, drywall and insulation that touched sewage
  • Always removed: food, medicine and cosmetics that touched sewage

The crew lists everything removed, with photos, for your insurance records.

Sewer Backups in Rentals, Condos and Businesses

In multi-unit buildings, a blocked main line can send sewage into several ground-floor units at once. In restaurants and other businesses, floor drains in kitchens and restrooms are common backup points. Each space is cleaned, disinfected and documented separately so owners, tenants, HOAs and property managers all have clear records. See condo water damage and commercial water damage.

How to Tell If the Problem Is Your Lateral or the City Main

If only your home is backing up, the blockage is most likely in your private lateral. If several neighbors are having backups at the same time, the problem may be in the public main, which the city handles. In that case, call the City of San Bruno's public works department to report it, then call for cleanup inside your home.

Sewage Odor After Cleanup

A lingering smell after a sewage backup usually means contaminated material is still somewhere it should not be: under flooring, inside a wall cavity, or in the crawl space. It can also mean a dry drain trap or a damaged seal is letting sewer gas into the house. Proper cleanup removes the source of the odor, not just covers it. HEPA air scrubbers and odor treatment help clear the air once contaminated materials are out. If an odor returns weeks later, call to have the area checked again.
