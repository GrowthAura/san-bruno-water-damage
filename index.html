What should you do first when water damage hits a San Bruno home?

Stop the water source, shut off power to wet areas, move valuables to a dry spot, take photos, then call (650) 822-6798 so extraction and drying start before mold sets in.

Most damage after a leak happens in the first 24 to 48 hours. Drywall wicks water upward, hardwood starts to cup, and the cool, foggy air on the Peninsula keeps everything damp longer than it would inland. These quick steps limit what needs to be torn out later:

  • Find the main water shutoff. In most San Bruno homes it sits near the front of the house, in the garage, or at the meter box by the street. Turn it clockwise until it stops.
  • Cut the power to wet rooms at the breaker panel, but only if you can reach the panel without standing in water.
  • Lift furniture legs onto foil or wood blocks and pull rugs, books and electronics off wet floors.
  • Photograph everything before anything is moved or thrown out: the source, the standing water, the walls and the contents.
  • Stay away from sewage. If the water came from a toilet, floor drain or sewer line, keep children and pets out of the area.
  • Call for extraction. Household fans and shop vacs can't pull water out of wall cavities, subfloors or crawl spaces.

Why Are San Bruno Homes Prone to Water Damage?

San Bruno is mostly a city of post-war homes. The typical house was built in the late 1950s, and more than half of local housing dates from the 1940s through the 1960s. That age shapes the kind of water damage people here deal with.

Aging Pipes and Sewer Lines

Galvanized steel supply pipes rust from the inside, narrowing until a joint fails. Cast iron drain lines crack, and older clay or cast iron sewer laterals let roots in. The city requires a sewer lateral inspection when a home is sold, and the private lateral from the house to the main is the owner's responsibility. A cracked or root-blocked lateral is one of the most common causes of sewage backups here.

Crawl Spaces Under Raised Homes

Many mid-century ranches in Crestmoor, Rollingwood and the western hills sit over crawl spaces rather than slabs. Rain runs downhill, collects under the house and sits there unseen. Weeks later the first sign is often a musty smell or soft flooring.

San Bruno Creek and Low-Lying Streets

Stormwater from much of the city drains to San Bruno Creek, which empties into the Bay near SFO. When heavy rain lines up with a high tide, the creek backs up. Belle Air and the streets around 7th Avenue and SR-380 have dealt with repeated flooding, and San Bruno Avenue near Huntington Avenue has closed during big storms.

Hillside Runoff

Neighborhoods built into the hills, including Crestmoor, Portola Highlands and Pacific Heights, send a lot of water downhill in a short time. Homes on the low side of the street, with garages or in-law units at grade, take the brunt of it.

Fog, Marine Layer and Slow Drying

The northern and western parts of San Bruno sit in the Peninsula fog belt. Cool, humid air means a wet wall or subfloor does not dry out by itself, and mold has more time to start. Proper drying here takes dehumidifiers and moisture checks, not open windows.

How the Water Damage Restoration Process Works

Whether it is a single bathroom leak or a whole-floor flood, the work follows the same core steps.

1. Emergency Call and Dispatch

2. Inspection and Moisture Mapping

The crew stops the source if it is still active, checks for electrical hazards, and uses moisture meters and thermal imaging to find out how far the water spread behind walls and under floors.

3. Water Extraction

Standing water is pumped and vacuumed out of floors, carpet, pad and crawl spaces. The more water removed now, the shorter the drying time.

4. Removal of Unsalvageable Materials

Wet pad, swollen baseboards, saturated insulation and drywall below the water line are removed when they cannot be dried. Anything touched by sewage is handled under Category 3 rules.

5. Structural Drying and Dehumidification

Air movers and dehumidifiers are set up and readings are taken each day until wood, drywall and subfloor reach normal moisture levels. This usually takes three to five days.

6. Cleaning, Sanitizing and Mold Prevention

Surfaces are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial products to stop mold and bacteria growth.

7. Repairs and Rebuild

Drywall, baseboards, flooring and paint are put back so the room looks the way it did before the loss.

What Are the Three Categories of Water Damage?

The IICRC S500 standard, the main industry reference for water damage work, sorts water by how contaminated it is. The category decides how the job is handled.

Category 1, clean water:

broken supply lines, burst pipes, overflowing sinks, rainwater. Lowest risk if handled quickly.

Category 2, gray water:

washing machine and dishwasher discharge, toilet overflow without waste, sump pump failures. Contains contaminants that can make people sick.

Category 3, black water:

sewage, creek and flood water from outside, and any water left standing long enough to grow bacteria. Porous materials it touches usually have to be removed.

Clean water turns into gray or black water when it sits. A pipe leak left for several days can become a Category 3 job, which is one more reason to start extraction early.

Will Homeowners Insurance Pay for Water Damage in California?

Most homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe or a failed water heater. Damage from slow leaks over time, poor maintenance or outside flooding is often excluded, and flood coverage usually needs a separate policy.

Every policy is different, so check yours or call your agent. What helps any claim is good documentation. On every job our crew records:

  • Photos of the source and damaged areas before work starts
  • Moisture readings taken each day of drying
  • A list of removed materials and affected contents
  • A written scope of the work completed

You can share this record with your adjuster. The decision on coverage always stays between you and your insurance company.

Storm Season Tips for San Bruno Homeowners

Most of San Bruno's flooding happens between November and March, when atmospheric river storms roll in off the Pacific. A little prep before the season starts goes a long way:

  • Clear gutters, downspouts and the storm drain in front of your house.
  • Pick up sandbags early. The city has historically offered them behind Fire Station 51 at 555 El Camino Real.
  • Check that crawl space vents and foundation drains are not blocked.
  • Test your sump pump if you have one, and keep a battery backup ready.
  • Know where your main water shutoff and breaker panel are.
  • If you live near San Bruno Creek, keep valuables off garage and ground floors during storm warnings.

How long does it take to dry a house after water damage?

Most homes take three to five days of drying. Crawl spaces, hardwood floors, sewage jobs and homes in foggy neighborhoods can take longer.

Can I clean up water damage myself?

+

Small clean-water spills on hard floors can be handled with towels and a wet vac. Water inside walls, under floors, in a crawl space or from sewage needs professional water damage cleanup and drying equipment to prevent mold.

Will my carpet need to be thrown out?

+

Carpet soaked with clean water can often be dried and saved, though the pad underneath is usually replaced. Carpet touched by sewage or floodwater has to be removed.
