What should you do when a water heater leaks in a San Bruno garage or closet?

Shut off the cold water valve on top of the tank, turn off the gas or breaker, keep clear of hot water, and call (650) 822-6798 for extraction and drying.

Most San Bruno homes keep the water heater in the garage, a hall closet or a utility area. A typical tank holds 40 to 50 gallons, and once it fails, the cold water line keeps refilling it. That means a leaking tank can keep putting water on your floor until the supply is shut off.

How to Shut Down a Leaking Water Heater

1. Turn Off the Water

Find the cold water inlet valve on the pipe at the top of the tank and turn it clockwise until it stops. If you cannot reach it, shut the main water valve to the house.

2. Turn Off the Energy Source

For a gas heater, turn the gas control knob to "off" or "pilot." For an electric heater, switch off its breaker.

3. Stay Safe

Water from the tank can be scalding. Keep children and pets away and let it cool before touching anything.

4. Take Photos

Photograph the tank, the leak and the wet area.

5. Call for Help

Common Appliance Leak Sources in San Bruno, CA

An appliance leak in San Bruno, CA usually comes from one of these:

  • Water heaters: corroded tanks, failed relief valves, loose fittings
  • Washing machines: burst or cracked supply hoses, drain hose leaks, a washing machine overflow from a clogged drain
  • Dishwashers: worn door seals, loose drain lines, failed inlet valves
  • Refrigerators: cracked ice maker lines, clogged defrost drains
  • Garbage disposals: cracked housings and loose connections under the sink
  • HVAC systems: clogged condensate lines and overflowing drip pans
  • Water softeners and filtration systems: loose fittings and failed valves

Why Slow Appliance Leaks Cause Big Damage

A burst hose is obvious. A slow leak is not. Dishwashers and refrigerators leak under cabinets and flooring where you cannot see. By the time a floor lifts or a cabinet base swells, water may have soaked the subfloor for weeks. In San Bruno's cool, damp climate, that is plenty of time for mold to start.

Preventing Appliance Leaks

  • Replace rubber washing machine hoses with braided steel hoses every five years
  • Install a drain pan under the water heater and washer
  • Add water leak sensors near appliances that alert your phone
  • Check under the sink and dishwasher every few months
  • Flush the water heater once a year to reduce sediment
  • Replace water heaters that are 10 to 15 years old before they fail
  • Keep refrigerator ice maker lines from kinking or rubbing

Water Heater and Appliance Leak FAQs

How long does a water heater last?

+

Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years. Rust at the base, rusty hot water or popping noises can mean it is near the end.

Is a leaking water heater dangerous?

+

The main risks are scalding water, electrical hazards and gas appliances getting wet. Shut it down and keep clear until it is checked.

Does insurance cover appliance leaks?

+

Sudden leaks are often covered. Slow leaks over time and the appliance itself are often excluded. Check your policy.

Can my kitchen cabinets be saved?

+

Solid wood cabinets often can be dried in place. Particleboard bases that have swollen usually need replacing.

Tank vs Tankless Water Heater Leaks

Tank water heaters usually fail at the bottom, where sediment builds up and the tank rusts through. When they go, they can release the full tank and keep refilling until the cold supply is shut off. Tankless water heaters do not store water, so a failure rarely floods a room, but their fittings, pressure relief valves and condensate lines can still leak slowly inside walls or closets. Both types should have their connections checked once a year.

Washing Machine Overflow: What Gets Damaged

A washing machine overflow can put a lot of water on the floor fast, especially if a supply hose bursts while nobody is home. In a garage, water spreads across the slab and into stored items. In an interior laundry room, it soaks flooring, baseboards and the bottom of drywall, and can reach the ceiling below on a second floor. Laundry water is usually treated as Category 2 because it carries soap, lint and organic matter, so cleanup includes sanitizing as well as drying.

Leak Sensors and Automatic Shutoff Valves

Two simple devices can turn a disaster into a minor cleanup:

  • Water leak sensors: small battery-powered pucks placed under sinks, behind toilets and next to water heaters and washers; they sound an alarm or send a phone alert when they detect water
  • Automatic shutoff valves: installed on the main water line or on individual appliances, they close automatically when a leak is detected

For homes that sit empty during the day, or second homes and rentals, these devices are some of the best protection you can buy.

Refrigerator and Ice Maker Line Leaks

The thin plastic or copper line that feeds an ice maker or water dispenser is easy to kink or crack when the fridge is moved. A slow drip behind the refrigerator can soak the subfloor for weeks before the flooring lifts. If you see warping in front of the fridge, pull it out and check the line.

Dishwasher Leaks Under Kitchen Cabinets

Dishwasher leaks are easy to miss because water spreads under the machine and the cabinets beside it. Signs include:

  • Flooring that lifts or darkens in front of the dishwasher
  • A musty smell when you open the sink cabinet
  • Swollen toe kicks or cabinet bottoms
  • Water on the floor after a wash cycle

The fix starts with repairing the dishwasher connection or door seal. Then the cabinets, subfloor and nearby walls are dried, often by removing toe kicks and forcing dry air underneath.

More Questions About Appliance Leaks

How do I know if my water heater is leaking or just condensing?

+

Condensation forms small drops on the outside of the tank, often when it first heats cold water. A real leak keeps growing, leaves rust stains, or comes from fittings, the relief valve or the bottom of the tank.

Should I replace my washing machine hoses?

+

Yes, if they are rubber or more than five years old. Braided steel hoses last longer and are far less likely to burst.

Can a refrigerator leak damage the floor below?

+

Yes. Slow leaks from ice maker lines can soak the subfloor and, on upper floors or in condos, reach the ceiling below.
