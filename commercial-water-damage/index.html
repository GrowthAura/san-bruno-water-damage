Commercial Water Damage: Key Facts

Here are the basics every business owner should know:

  • Commercial water damage is any water loss in a business property, from a small leak to a full flood.
  • Most commercial property policies cover sudden water damage, but not flooding from outside water without separate coverage.
  • Business interruption coverage may help with lost income while you are closed. Check your policy.
  • Mold can start growing within 24 to 72 hours, so drying should start right away.
  • Drop ceilings, carpet tiles and drywall are the materials most often damaged.
  • Documenting damage before cleanup protects your claim.

Water Damage in Offices and Server Rooms

Water near computers, servers and electrical equipment needs extra care. Power to affected areas is shut down, equipment is moved out of wet areas, and drying is planned so dust and humidity do not harm sensitive electronics.

Preventing Commercial Water Damage

  • Have sprinkler systems inspected on schedule
  • Inspect roofs before the rainy season
  • Install leak sensors near water heaters, ice machines and restrooms
  • Train staff to find and use water shutoff valves
  • Keep floor drains clear, especially in kitchens
  • Keep stock and equipment off the floor in storage rooms

Planning Ahead: A Water Emergency Plan for Your Business

Every business should know who to call and what to do before water hits. Keep the main water shutoff location posted, train key staff on how to use it, store (650) 822-6798 in your emergency contacts, and keep a list of critical equipment that must be protected first.

Sprinkler Line Breaks

Fire sprinkler systems hold water under pressure, and when a pipe or head breaks, water comes out fast and keeps coming until the system is shut down. Breaks happen from accidental impact, corrosion, or freezing in rare cold spots. The building's fire sprinkler contractor should handle shutting down and restoring the system. Once the water is off, extraction and drying begin right away, often across several rooms or floors at once.

Drop Ceilings and Carpet Tiles

Offices often have suspended ceiling tiles and carpet tiles. Wet ceiling tiles usually sag and stain and are replaced. Carpet tiles can sometimes be lifted, cleaned and dried individually, which is faster than dealing with broadloom carpet. The space above the drop ceiling and under raised floors also needs to be checked for trapped moisture.

Getting Back to Business

Once drying is complete, the focus shifts to repairs and reopening. Repairs can often be scheduled in phases, so finished areas return to use while others are still being worked on. Keeping customers, staff and tenants informed about which areas are open helps the transition go smoothly.
