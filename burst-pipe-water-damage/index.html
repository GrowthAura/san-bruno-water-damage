Why do pipes burst in older San Bruno homes built in the 1950s?

Many were built with galvanized steel supply pipes that rust from the inside. After 60 or more years, joints and threads thin out and split under normal water pressure.

San Bruno grew fast after World War II, and much of the city's housing went up between the 1940s and 1960s. Back then, galvanized steel was the standard for water lines. It works for decades, but the zinc coating wears away, the steel corrodes, and the pipe narrows and weakens. A galvanized pipe leak often starts small at a threaded joint in a crawl space or behind a wall, then fails all at once.

Signs a Pipe Is About to Fail

Catching a weak pipe early can save thousands in damage. Watch for:

  • Low water pressure in several fixtures
  • Brown or rusty water when you first open a tap
  • Small damp spots or stains on walls and ceilings
  • A sound of running water when everything is shut off
  • A musty smell from a closet, cabinet or crawl space
  • A water bill that jumps with no change in use

The old pipe warning signs guide covers this in more detail.

What to Do the Moment a Pipe Bursts

1. Shut Off the Main Water

In most San Bruno homes the main valve is near the front of the house, in the garage, or at the meter box near the sidewalk. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

2. Kill Power to Wet Areas

If water is near outlets, light fixtures or the electrical panel, switch off the breakers for those rooms, but only if you can reach the panel without standing in water.

3. Open Faucets

Open a low faucet to drain remaining water out of the lines and reduce pressure.

4. Move Belongings

Get electronics, rugs, papers and furniture out of the wet area.

5. Take Photos

Photograph the burst pipe, the water and the affected rooms before cleanup starts.

Where Pipes Burst in San Bruno Homes

Under Raised Floors

In raised ranch homes in Crestmoor and Rollingwood, supply lines run through the crawl space. A failure there can soak the soil and framing long before anyone notices. See crawl space water damage.

Inside Walls

Pipes inside exterior walls and between bathrooms can leak for days, soaking drywall and insulation from the inside.

Above Ceilings

Two-story homes and condos in Shelter Creek and around Tanforan often have supply lines above ceilings. A burst there brings water down through light fixtures and drywall.

Under Slabs

Homes on slabs in the flats can have pipe failures under the floor. These show up as warm spots, cracks or wet flooring. See slab leak water damage.

At Fixtures and Appliances

Supply hoses on toilets, sinks, washing machines and refrigerators fail often, especially rubber hoses that are more than a few years old.

Galvanized, Copper and PEX: What Fails Most

  • Galvanized steel: common in homes built before the 1960s. Rusts from the inside and fails at threaded joints.
  • Copper: used from the 1960s on. Lasts longer but can develop pinhole leaks, especially near fittings.
  • Plastic supply hoses: under sinks and toilets. Cheap ones crack or burst with age.
  • PEX: newer and flexible. Failures usually happen at fittings or where it was damaged during installation.

If your home has had more than one pipe failure, a plumber can tell you whether a full repipe makes sense. That decision is yours, but drying and repairs come first.

Insurance and Burst Pipes

Most homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental pipe bursts. Damage from a slow leak that went on for a long time is often excluded. Every policy is different, so check yours. The crew documents every job with photos and moisture logs to support your claim.

The First 48 Hours After a Burst Pipe

Knowing what happens in the first two days helps you understand why speed matters so much.

The First Hour

Water spreads across floors and starts soaking into carpet pad, baseboards and the bottom of drywall. Shutting off the main valve and calling for help are the most important steps.

Hours 1 to 6

Drywall wicks water upward. Wood floors begin to absorb water at the edges. Water may reach the ceiling below or the crawl space. Extraction during this window saves the most material.

Hours 6 to 24

Carpet pad is usually saturated. Particleboard in cabinets may start to swell. Paint can bubble. Odors begin. Drying equipment should be running by now.

Days 1 to 2

Without drying, hardwood begins to cup and mold can start on wet drywall and wood. With drying underway, readings should be dropping each day.

Questions to Ask Your Plumber After a Pipe Bursts

Once the water is under control, a plumber repairs the pipe. These questions help you decide what comes next:

  • What caused the pipe to fail: age, corrosion, a bad fitting or pressure?
  • Is the rest of the pipe in that area in similar condition?
  • How much original galvanized plumbing is left in the house?
  • Would a pressure regulator help protect the pipes?
  • Do you recommend a repair now and a repipe later, or a repipe now?

The answers help you avoid another burst in a different spot a few months later.

Protecting Your Home From the Next Burst Pipe

A few habits make a big difference in older San Bruno homes:

  • Know where your main shutoff is, and make sure it turns easily
  • Replace rubber supply hoses on toilets, sinks and washers with braided steel
  • Have water pressure checked; pressure that is too high stresses old pipes
  • Look in the crawl space or garage once a season for rust, drips and damp spots
  • Install leak sensors near water heaters, washers and under sinks
  • Turn off the main water when you leave home for more than a few days

These steps take little time and can prevent thousands of dollars in damage, and they matter most in homes where the original plumbing has never been replaced.
