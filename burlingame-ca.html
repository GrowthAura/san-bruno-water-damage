Why Older Burlingame Homes Need a Different Approach

Basements and Below-Grade Rooms

Basements sit below the surrounding soil. When the ground is saturated after days of rain, groundwater pushes through foundation walls, floor cracks and the joint where the floor meets the wall. Basements with laundry rooms, workshops or finished living space can lose flooring, drywall and stored belongings. Basement flooding Burlingame homeowners see most often happens late in the rainy season, after the soil has been soaking for weeks.

Plaster Walls

Plaster and lath walls hold water differently than drywall. They can dry well if handled carefully, but they can also lose their bond and crack. The crew dries plaster slowly and checks it before any repair decision.

Original Hardwood Floors

Old-growth oak and fir floors are worth saving. Caught early, they can usually be dried in place with floor mats before they cup or buckle. See hardwood floor water damage.

Aging Plumbing and Sewer Laterals

Galvanized supply lines, cast iron drains and clay sewer laterals are common in older homes. Roots and corrosion lead to leaks and sewage backups, often into basements first.

Large Trees

Burlingame's mature street trees are a big part of the city, but their roots can crack sewer laterals and foundation drains, and falling limbs can damage roofs during storms.

Older Home Basement Flooding: What to Do

Older home basement flooding needs a careful response:

  • Stay out of the basement if water is near outlets, the electrical panel, the furnace or the water heater
  • If it is safe, shut off power to the basement at the breaker
  • Move belongings off the floor and away from walls
  • Do not pump out a deeply flooded basement too fast if the soil outside is still saturated, because pressure from the outside can damage walls

Drying Older Materials Without Damaging Them

Older materials need a measured approach. Drying plaster, old-growth wood and original trim too fast can crack, warp or split them. The crew:

  • Measures moisture in each material and compares it to dry areas of the same home
  • Controls temperature and humidity so materials release water gradually
  • Uses floor mats and wall cavity drying to save finishes instead of tearing them out
  • Documents readings daily so you can see progress

The goal is always to save what can be saved and replace only what cannot.

Preventing Water Damage in Burlingame Homes

  • Have older sewer laterals inspected with a camera, especially under large trees

  • Replace old rubber hoses on washers and toilets with braided steel
  • Check the basement and crawl space after every big storm

Basement Waterproofing After a Flood

Once a Burlingame basement is dry, the next question is how to keep water out. Common options include:

  • Repairing cracks in foundation walls and floors
  • Improving grading and downspouts so water flows away from the house
  • Installing or upgrading a sump pump with battery backup
  • Adding an interior drain system along the base of the walls
  • Applying a waterproof coating on the inside of foundation walls
  • Installing a backwater valve on the sewer line

The right combination depends on where the water comes in. Watching the basement during the next storm, and noting exactly where water appears, helps a contractor recommend the right fix.

Finished Basements and Mold

Finished basements with carpet, drywall and insulation are the most likely to grow mold after a flood. If a finished basement takes on water, the bottom of the drywall and the insulation behind it usually need to come out so the wall can dry. Rebuilding with moisture-resistant materials, such as tile or vinyl flooring and mold-resistant drywall, reduces the damage the next time.

Large Trees, Roots and Sewer Backups in Burlingame

Burlingame is known for its tall street trees, especially the eucalyptus and elms that line many older neighborhoods. Those trees are part of what makes the city special, but their roots go looking for water and can find it inside old clay and cast iron sewer laterals. Over time, roots fill the pipe, catch paper and debris, and cause backups. If your home has a large tree near the line and you notice slow drains or gurgling, a camera inspection can show whether roots are the problem. Fixing it early is far cheaper than cleaning up after sewage reaches a basement or ground-floor bathroom.
