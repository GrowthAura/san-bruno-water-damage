What causes ceilings to leak in San Bruno during wind-driven rain?

Strong storm winds off the Pacific push rain sideways under shingles, around flashing, vents and chimneys, and through gaps that stay dry in normal rain.

San Bruno sits just east of the coastal hills, and winter storms often arrive with strong southwest winds. Rain that would normally run off a roof gets driven up under shingles and into joints. Older composition roofs, worn flashing around chimneys and skylights, and clogged gutters are the usual weak spots. Water then soaks into attic insulation and spreads across the top of the ceiling drywall before it ever shows below.

Other Sources of Ceiling Water Damage

Not every wet ceiling is a roof leak. Common sources in San Bruno homes and condos include:

  • A bathroom or kitchen above: leaking shower pans, toilet seals or supply lines
  • A unit above in a condo or apartment building
  • Supply pipes running through the ceiling
  • HVAC condensation lines and drip pans in the attic
  • Water heaters in attics or upper closets
  • Ice maker and refrigerator lines on an upper floor

Finding the true source matters, because drying a ceiling that keeps getting wet just wastes time.

Warning Signs of Roof Leak Water Damage in San Bruno, CA

Watch for these signs of roof leak water damage in San Bruno, CA:

  • A water stain on ceiling drywall, often with a brown ring
  • Paint bubbling or peeling on the ceiling or upper walls
  • Drywall that sags or feels soft
  • Dripping from light fixtures, vents or smoke detectors
  • A musty smell in upstairs rooms or closets
  • Damp or matted insulation in the attic
  • Mold spots on the ceiling, especially in corners

Is a Sagging Ceiling Dangerous?

Yes. Drywall holding water can collapse without warning, bringing down wet insulation and debris. If a ceiling is bulging:

  • Keep everyone out of the room
  • Turn off power to lights and fans in that area
  • Put a bucket under the lowest point
  • Do not poke it unless you are sure of what you are doing, and stand to the side if you do

How Ceiling Water Damage Is Repaired

Find the Source

The crew traces the water with moisture meters and thermal imaging from the ceiling up to the attic, roof or unit above.

Stop Further Water

A temporary tarp is set on the roof if needed, or the plumbing source is shut off until a repair is made.

Remove Wet Insulation

Soaked insulation in the attic is removed so the ceiling and framing can dry.

Dry the Ceiling and Attic

Dehumidifiers and air movers dry the drywall, joists and roof sheathing. Small holes may be drilled to release trapped water.

Remove Damaged Drywall

Drywall that has sagged, crumbled or grown mold is cut out.

Treat for Mold

Affected areas are treated with antimicrobial products.

Repair and Paint

New drywall is hung, textured to match and painted.

Ceilings in Condos and Multi-Unit Buildings

In Shelter Creek and other multi-unit buildings, a wet ceiling usually means water from the unit above. The source unit, your unit and sometimes common areas all need to be checked. Who pays often depends on the HOA rules and where the leak started. See condo water damage.

Plaster Ceilings in Older Homes

Some older homes near downtown and in San Bruno Park still have plaster ceilings. Plaster holds water differently than drywall and can lose its bond to the lath above. These ceilings need careful drying and checking before repairs.

Preventing Roof Leaks

  • Clean gutters and downspouts before the rainy season
  • Have the roof checked every few years, and after big storms
  • Replace cracked or missing shingles and worn flashing
  • Keep trees trimmed back from the roof
  • Check attic insulation for damp spots after heavy rain
  • Make sure attic vents and bath fans vent outside, not into the attic

Ceiling Water Damage FAQs

Can a water-stained ceiling be dried instead of replaced?

+

Often, yes. If the drywall is still solid and caught early, it can be dried, sealed with stain-blocking primer and repainted.

How do I know if it is a roof leak or a plumbing leak?

+

If it only leaks during rain, it is usually the roof. If it leaks any time, or when a fixture above is used, it is usually plumbing.

Will insurance pay for a roof leak?

+

Sudden damage from a storm is often covered. Leaks from an old, worn roof are often excluded as maintenance. Check your policy.

How long does ceiling repair take?

+

Drying takes a few days. Drywall repair, texture and paint usually take another one to three days.

Attic Moisture and Hidden Roof Leaks

Not every roof leak shows up as a stain right away. In many homes, water first soaks into attic insulation, which acts like a sponge. It can hold water for weeks before the drywall below finally shows a stain. By then, roof sheathing, rafters and ceiling joists may have mold. If you have had a big storm and notice a musty smell upstairs, or see a faint ring on the ceiling, check the attic with a flashlight. Look for dark or damp insulation, water marks on the underside of the roof, and rusty nail tips poking through the sheathing.

Temporary Roof Tarping After a Storm

When a storm damages the roof, the first goal is to stop more water from getting in. A temporary tarp, secured over the damaged area, buys time until a roofer can make a permanent repair. A good tarp:

  • Covers well past the damaged area on all sides
  • Is secured with boards, not just nails through the tarp
  • Runs over the ridge so water cannot get under the top edge
  • Is checked after each new storm

Never climb onto a wet or steep roof yourself during a storm. Call for help.

Skylights, Chimneys and Flashing

The most common leak points on Peninsula roofs are where something passes through or meets the roof. Skylights have seals that dry out and crack. Chimneys have flashing that pulls away as mortar ages. Vent pipes have rubber boots that split in the sun. Valleys where two roof slopes meet carry a lot of water and can leak if debris builds up. If your ceiling stain is near one of these spots, that is the first place to check.

Ceiling Water Damage in Rentals

If you rent and see a ceiling stain or drip, tell your landlord or property manager in writing right away and take photos. Fast reporting helps get the leak fixed before it spreads and protects you if belongings are damaged.

Ceiling Leaks From Bathrooms Above

One of the most common causes of ceiling stains that are not roof related is a bathroom on the floor above. Leaks usually come from:

  • A worn wax ring under the toilet
  • Cracked grout or failed caulk around the tub or shower
  • A leaking shower valve inside the wall
  • A cracked or poorly sloped shower pan
  • Overflow from a tub or sink

These leaks often show up only when the fixture is used, so if a stain grows after showers, start there. Fixing the source and drying the ceiling cavity prevents mold above the drywall.

More Questions About Wet Ceilings

Should I drill a hole in a bulging ceiling?

+

Only if you are confident it is safe. A small hole at the lowest point can release water into a bucket and reduce the chance of collapse, but stand to the side and turn off power to nearby lights first. If unsure, wait for the crew.

Can mold grow above the ceiling?

+

Yes. Wet insulation and the top side of drywall can grow mold where you cannot see it. Drying the ceiling cavity and checking the attic prevents that.

Will the stain come back after painting?

+

If the ceiling is fully dry and sealed with a stain-blocking primer first, water stains should not bleed through new paint.
